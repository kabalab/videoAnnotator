# NYC Main Sessions Guide: NYC19, NYC23, NYC26

## NYC19: Love God, Love Others (Phoenix, July 2019)

### Sermon 1: WORSHIP

**Preacher:** Amena Brown

**Speaker background:** Amena Brown is an Atlanta-based spoken-word poet, writer, and performing artist whose work blends storytelling, rhyme, and humor. She is a Spelman College graduate and the author of books including Breaking Old Rhythms and How to Fix a Broken Record, and has performed and spoken widely at conferences and cultural events. She lives in Atlanta with her husband, DJ Opdiggy, and often collaborates creative and DJ-related performance with her stage work.

**Message focus:** Brown taught that worship is not only something people do but something God created people to be, drawing on themes of loving God and on John 4's living-water encounter (referenced in the closing-session recap). She used hip-hop and DJ imagery, asking whether God could be like a DJ who takes broken "breaks," mistakes, and mismatched pieces and remakes them into something new, and contrasted identity rooted in social media's "cool kids table" with identity rooted in God. She performed spoken-word poetry (including material she traced to her mother's influence and pieces such as "Roots and Wings") and a DJ demonstration with DJ Opdiggy, pressing students to see worship as exhaling the breath of God rather than performing for approval.

---

### Sermon 2: SURRENDER

**Preacher:** Stretch Dean

**Speaker background:** Stretch Dean is a longtime Nazarene youth leader and pastor. As of a 2018 NYI Connect profile he served as Eastern Field Youth Coordinator and lead pastor of Wollaston Church of the Nazarene and attended NYC as a student in 1987. He was an announced plenary speaker for NYC 2019.

**Message focus:** Dean preached surrender from Romans 12:1-2 ("do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind"), arguing that knowing God's love should lead to full surrender, not half-measures ("side hugs"). He used personal stories (including an Oreo/plumber illustration remembered in the final-session recap) and challenged students to stand if they were "all in," culminating in a memorable image of running, covered in mess, into the Father's arms. The call was that what you keep for yourself stays only what it is, but what you surrender to Jesus can be multiplied.

---

### Sermon 3: SERVE

**Preacher:** Danielle Strickland

**Speaker background:** Danielle Strickland is a Canadian speaker, author, and justice advocate who previously served as a Salvation Army officer and church planter for many years. She has written multiple books on freedom, hope, and discipleship and speaks internationally on living kingdom love in practical, disruptive ways.

**Message focus:** Strickland taught that loving God requires loving others, landing especially in Mark 10:45 and the idea of seeing people, and yourself, through Jesus' "kingdom lens" / "love-colored glasses." She stressed that Jesus sees students not primarily through their mistakes but through a lens of love, and that serving others begins with receiving that love yourself ("you are enough"). She illustrated costly, concrete love with stories (including a Starbucks/security encounter and crib/value imagery recalled in the closing recap) and challenged the room to put those glasses on and serve.

---

### Sermon 4: CHURCH

**Preacher:** Justin Skeesuck and Patrick Gray (co-speakers)

**Speaker background:** Justin Skeesuck and Patrick Gray are lifelong friends, Nazarene university alumni (Point Loma Nazarene University and Northwest Nazarene University), and the authors/subjects of I'll Push You, the story of Gray pushing Skeesuck, who lives with a progressive neuromuscular disease, 500 miles on the Camino de Santiago. They speak widely on friendship, faith, and embodied care for others.

**Message focus:** Their session held up the Camino journey as a living picture of the church: not only words about love but action ("I'll push you"), alongside Scripture including John and Matthew 25's call to care for "the least of these." The closing-session recap framed their message as showing what church looks like when success is measured by feeding, visiting, giving drink, and pushing one another. Regional Youth Coordinator Justin Pickard later wrote that their NYC19 message on "to love God is to love others" drew a standing ovation from about 9,000 people.

---

### Sermon 5: HOLINESS

**Preacher:** Shawna Songer Gaines

**Speaker background:** Shawna Songer Gaines (D.Min., Lipscomb University) has served as lead pastor of Trevecca Community Church in Nashville, Tennessee. She is a welcomed speaker for worship and leadership gatherings and co-author of works including A Seat at the Table and Kings and Presidents. At NYC19 she introduced herself as pastor of Trevecca Community Church and wife of Tim Gaines.

**Message focus:** Asked to preach holiness, Gaines rejected holiness as a distant "arrival point" you fake by looking put-together. Using Psalm 51 and David's undoing, she described sin as the unraveling of God's good creation and holiness as being made new in the image of God, taking off the mask, letting yourself come undone so God can remake you. She illustrated restoration with a personal story of an old Isuzu Trooper her grandfather rebuilt, comparing God's rebuilding work to knitting people back together. The challenge was to stop pretending and enter the undoing that leads to new creation.

---

### Sermon 6: CALLED

**Preacher:** Dr. Gustavo A. Crocker ("Dr. Gus")

**Speaker background:** Dr. Gustavo A. Crocker, born in Guatemala, is the 41st General Superintendent of the Church of the Nazarene (elected 2013), the first Central American to hold that office. A licensed architect and Fulbright Scholar (M.A., University of Cincinnati; Ph.D., Regent University), he previously directed Eurasia Region work and held leadership roles with Nazarene Compassionate Ministries, Compassion International, and World Relief.

**Message focus:** Crocker preached calling as living love in costly, even "crazy" obedience, asking whether students would do radical things not to impress people but to save people, including the image of cutting a hole in a roof to get someone to Jesus. He urged them not to get so focused on the destination that they miss the journey, warning against being part of the crowd that keeps people from seeing Jesus. Many students responded via the conference app that God was calling them. A conference quote attributed to him on the NYC2019 recap site: "There is nothing that can keep you away from the love of Jesus Christ!"

---

### Sermon 7: GO

**Preacher:** Eric Samuel Timm

**Speaker background:** Eric Samuel Timm is a communicator, author, and artist who often pairs live painting with preaching. He has served as lead/pastoring voice at Summit Church in Saint Paul, Minnesota, and speaks widely to churches, students, and leaders about hope and seeing God's truth freshly. He returned as a featured speaker at later NYC events as well.

**Message focus:** Closing NYC19, Timm reviewed the week's arc (worship, surrender, serve, church, holiness, call) and anchored "go" in communion and living love after the conference. He spoke to students eager to leave and those afraid to go home, urging them to remember what God had done and to carry loving God and loving others into ordinary life. The session framed departure not as the end of the experience but as the start of living the love they had received.

## NYC23: Overflow (Tampa, July 2023)

Theme verse: Romans 15:13.

### Sermon 1: OVERFLOW

**Preacher:** LaMorris Crawford

**Speaker background:** LaMorris Crawford grew up on Chicago's South Side amid poverty and violence, encountered Christ at 19, and became the first in his family to attend college, earning undergraduate and graduate degrees from Olivet Nazarene University. With his wife Megan he co-founded Covenant Culture Ministries and has served as an NFL team chaplain (Cincinnati Bengals; later Carolina Panthers).

**Message focus:** Opening Overflow, Crawford preached hope in Christ and life in the Spirit using water imagery from Genesis 1:1-2 (Spirit hovering over the waters), John 10:10, and Isaiah 59:19. He taught that a place without water has no life, a heart without the Spirit has no hope, and God speaks to purpose, urging students not to let others' opinions become their reality but to live in God's reality. He shared from his own story (including basketball as escape) and challenged dry places in their lives to receive the Spirit's overflow.

---

### Sermon 2: SPRING

**Preacher:** Carly Bartlett

**Speaker background:** Carly Bartlett is an ordained elder in the Church of the Nazarene, NNU alumna (Communication; MBA), and M.Div. graduate of Nazarene Theological Seminary (2022). She has served on pastoral staff in the Boise/Nampa, Idaho area (including Eagle Nazarene and Nampa College Church), teaches public speaking, and sensed a call to preach while attending NYC 2011.

**Message focus:** Bartlett preached God's renewing grace through the Samaritan woman at the well (John 4:7-15), connecting clenched-fist identity and performance (including a personal "gold cup" / piano competition story) to the woman's shame and thirst. She insisted Jesus meets people where they are, will not leave them stranded, and pours living water so they can pour into others. The challenge was to open clenched fists, receive grace, and overflow hope into community.

---

### Sermon 3: RAIN

**Preacher:** Chris Simning

**Speaker background:** Chris Simning is a full-time Christian speaker based in the Gilbert/Higley, Arizona area. Born with cerebral palsy and later stricken with a rare muscle/nerve disease that left him in a wheelchair for years, he experienced healing that enabled him to finish college and earn a master's in clinical psychology from Azusa Pacific University. He founded Chris Simning Ministries to speak on hope, brokenness, and God's provision.

**Message focus:** Using rain imagery and Isaiah 45:8-9 (and baptism language from Romans 6), Simning asked students to name the "puddles" they avoid (places of mess or shame) and taught that Jesus does not merely cover puddles but wants them and can use them. He insisted students' lives matter and that God works in mysterious ways so they can flourish amid brokenness. Response questions asked what their puddle was and what surrender meant after the message.

---

### Sermon 4: CURRENT

**Preacher:** Rich Villodas

**Speaker background:** Rich Villodas is Brooklyn-born lead pastor of New Life Fellowship in Queens, New York (since 2013), a multiracial church representing many nations. He holds degrees from Nyack College and Alliance Theological Seminary, speaks with Emotionally Healthy Discipleship, and authored The Deeply Formed Life, Good and Beautiful and Kind, and The Narrow Path.

**Message focus:** Villodas taught holiness as inside-out love rather than outside-in rule-keeping, using Mark 7 (what defiles comes from within) and 1 Corinthians 1:2. He warned that you can keep a Christian checklist and still be far from God, and that identity must be rooted in Jesus, not a single experience. Using a tea-bag analogy (dipping vs. soaking), he urged students to dwell in God's presence so holiness spills from heart to hands into a broken world.

---

### Sermon 5: RIVER

**Preacher:** Jason Ballard

**Speaker background:** Jason Ballard is lead pastor of The Way Church in Vancouver, British Columbia, and a host of Alpha's Youth Film Series. He works with Alpha and Canadian church networks creating resources for churches and youth workers and lives in Vancouver with his wife Rachael and their children.

**Message focus:** Ballard connected Romans 15:13's overflow to mission: God fills people with hope so they can share hope, as students who are missionaries in their schools. He used Luke 19:1-10 (Zacchaeus) to show Jesus seeking outsiders and producing repentant generosity, and challenged listeners to leave margin to bless others, care for the vulnerable, pray for non-Christian friends, and be a blessing among them.

---

### Sermon 6: WATERFALL

**Preacher:** Dr. Carla D. Sunberg

**Speaker background:** Dr. Carla Sunberg is the 43rd General Superintendent of the Church of the Nazarene (elected 2017). A missionary kid born in Germany, she and her husband Chuck served 13 years as pioneer missionaries in the former Soviet Union, later pastored in Fort Wayne, served as East Ohio co-district superintendents, and she previously served as president of Nazarene Theological Seminary. She holds nursing and theological degrees including a Ph.D. in historical theology.

**Message focus:** From Exodus 38:8 (the bronze basin made from mirrors of women serving at the tent of meeting), Sunberg compared ancient mirrors to today's phones as tools of self-projection and called students to surrender self-focus. She taught standing under the Spirit's "waterfall" / overflow so they are cleansed and empowered for life and calling, not merely momentarily inspired.

---

### Sermon 7: OVERFLOW

**Preacher:** Eric Samuel Timm

**Speaker background:** See Sermon 7 (NYC19). At NYC23 he again identified himself as a pastor in Saint Paul, Minnesota, and returned as a closing voice for Overflow.

**Message focus:** Timm warned against believing "the story's over" when hope leaks out, using a cracked vase illustration and Luke 6:45 (mouth speaking from the overflow of the heart). He insisted living water is not for hoarding: from Acts 1:8, students are witnesses from near to far ("it ain't over because the Overflow is just beginning"). The charge was to carry overflow home into every part of life.

## NYC26: Wonder (Salt Lake City, July 2026)

Theme verse: Psalm 65:8.

### Sermon 1: WONDER

**Preacher:** Gerald Fadayomi

**Speaker background:** Gerald Fadayomi is founding/senior pastor of Home Church Roswell (Atlanta area), a church plant gathering in community spaces. A former student pastor with North Point Ministries, he speaks widely to churches and youth events and is the author of books including Before You Go, Starting Now, and When Life Gives You Lemons. He and his wife Kiley have four daughters.

**Message focus:** Opening Wonder, Fadayomi centered Colossians 1:15-23 (Christ as image of the invisible God, Creator, Sustainer, and Redeemer) and pressed the difference between knowing about God and knowing God personally. Students were asked "Who is Jesus?" and "Who is Jesus to me?" and invited to see wonder not only in the universe but in themselves as image-bearers.

---

### Sermon 2: ASCEND

**Preacher:** Gabriela ("Gabby") Rodriguez

**Speaker background:** Gabriela Rodriguez is lead pastor of Trinity Church of the Nazarene in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma. She was the Thursday-night plenary preacher for NYC26.

**Message focus:** Rodriguez preached from 1 Kings 19, where Elijah flees Jezebel, collapses under a broom tree, is fed by an angel, and is told to "get up and eat" for the journey to Horeb. She taught that God invites people to ascend: faith can be one small step before you feel ready; God still calls people upward into his purposes. Students were urged to choose which voice stays when many voices speak.

---

### Sermon 3: FORM

**Preacher:** Kelvin Crosby

**Speaker background:** Kelvin Crosby, known as the DeafBlind Potter, is a San Diego-based potter, speaker, and accessibility advocate living with Usher syndrome. Through DeafBlind Potter / Living Beyond the Label he speaks on perseverance, addiction and suicide prevention, and being centered in God's hands while throwing pottery live; he is also founder/CEO of Smart Guider (See Me Cane).

**Message focus:** While forming pottery on stage, Crosby taught God as Master Potter who forms people, even through hard things, when they remain centered in his hands. He encouraged worship, Scripture, and prayer as basic Christian practices and left students with the image that collapse is not the end of the story.

---

### Sermon 4: AWAKEN

**Preacher:** Eric Samuel Timm

**Speaker background:** See Sermon 7 (NYC19). At NYC26 he again combined teaching with live art.

**Message focus:** Timm preached holiness/sanctification as preparation for life after the mountaintop, syncing one's heartbeat with God's through valleys as well as peaks. He painted during worship and flipped the painting to reveal mountain and valley connected, calling students to awaken into life with God in the ups and downs, not only the conference high.

---

### Sermon 5: BELONG

**Preacher:** Alison Springer

**Speaker background:** Alison Springer is a Calgary-based youth communications specialist, professional speaker, and founder of Greater Than Communications and the Young Women of Power Conference. She has decades of experience with church youth, schools, and high-needs youth programs in Western Canada and previously served in youth ministry at Calgary East Church of the Nazarene.

**Message focus:** In a message Life Church recorded as titled "Operation One Flock," Springer taught from John 10:11-16 that Jesus the Good Shepherd gathers where the devil isolates, gives abundant life where the enemy steals, and lays down his life for the sheep. She framed post-conference life as a "preparation for landing," applying what was learned in community rather than crashing alone, and urged students to stay locked into the flock.

---

### Sermon 6: PURSUE

**Preacher:** Dr. Gustavo A. Crocker

**Speaker background:** See Sermon 6 (NYC19). At NYC26 he invited students to call him "Uncle Gus."

**Message focus:** Preaching a "Christmas in July" message from Luke 1:26-38, Crocker compared students to Mary and the manger: highly favored and chosen though ordinary and unlikely. Pursuit of God, he taught, begins with availability ("here I am") because God comes not because people are able but because they are available; the Spirit transforms the ordinary into the extraordinary.

---

### Sermon 7: IMAGINE

**Preacher:** Shane Lima

**Speaker background:** Shane Lima is lead pastor of Ambassadors Church of the Nazarene in Rhode Island (New England District). He also served among the NYC pastoral/host team at NYC23 and closed NYC26.

**Message focus:** Lima framed the end of NYC as the beginning of living as God's holy people, salt and light (Matthew 5), carrying holiness into churches, districts, and schools without fear. Leaning into Wesleyan-Holiness roots, he invited students to imagine a future shaped by encounter with God and declared, "The future is bright, because this generation belongs to the Lord," before communion and closing worship.
